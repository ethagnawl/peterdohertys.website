# [Advent of Watchmen - 2025 - Issue #1](https://amzn.to/4saYooy)

This is part one of my 2025 _Advent of Watchmen_ series, which will run from 12/12/2025-12/24/2025.

This will be the fourth year I've done this and the fifth time I've read the complete series. I wish I could remember where I'd initially gotten the idea to do it as an "advent" but it's not an original idea of mine.

My plan is write a brief blog post about each issue, answering a series of questions each day. This format is stolen from iFanboy's _Pick of the Week_ "Patron Pick" segment -- which Ron Richards just recently admitted was stolen from a different podcast [citation needed].

I've never tried a writing project like this or written anything critical about media in a very long time, so I don't know how this is going to go but I am going to be kind to myself. If I'm able to get a post out each day during the advent, then I'll consider this project a success.

These posts will contain spoilers and may contain affiliate links.

Watchmen doesn't need an introduction but, just in case, it's a seminal work released in 1986 by Alan Moore, Dave Gibbons and John Higgins. The collected edition is the best selling graphic novel of all time, it's never gone out of print and was part of the Class of '86 (also included _Batman: The Dark Knight Returns_ and _Daredevil: Born Again_) which was a renaissance in comics and, for better or for worse, when comics _grew up_.

### What surprised you most about this issue?
There's no hand-holding. The first balloon (tire treaded ...) is iconic but I'd taken for granted that it's _the first panel_ in the series.

Despite its retro-futurism, this is the 1980s that will be more intimately familiar to most urbanites than the "slick talking", _Wall Street_, _Miami Vice_, kooky fluorescent one that's depicted in most media. It's gritty, cynical, sleazy and there is blood running in the streets (petty crime, crack, AIDS, peep shows in Times Square, etc.). It's also a very fraught political climate (micro and macro) which we're subtly introduced to by way of graffiti, posters and snippets of conversation. The whole issue is a masterclass in _show; don't tell_.

### Which single panel stood out most?
Rorschach climbing into The Comedian's apartment. It's the first time we see his mask, it almost feels like he's looking through the book and into the reader and the six panel drawing is stunningly beautiful.

### What did this issue go on to influence? Alternatively, what was this issue influenced by?
I see the fingerprints of this issue's city-as-canyon treatments (depth and dynamism) in the generation of books which followed. I don't have any specific examples in mind but you can imagine the panel of The Comedian falling out-and-away being replaced with Batman swooping in-and-towards and you've got the stuff of 90s splash pages and covers.
